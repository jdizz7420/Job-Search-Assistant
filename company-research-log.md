## Company Notes

### HubSpot — 2026-07-23

**Business fundamentals (4):** HubSpot posted 23% revenue growth in Q1 2026 and hit its 2027 margin target a year early. It holds the #1 spot in marketing automation at ~35% share. Q2 guidance shows deceleration to 16-18% YoY, attributed to April pricing/packaging changes — a minor caveat, not a red flag.

**Culture (3):** Glassdoor sits at 3.4/5, down 4% over the past year, with 54% recommending it to a friend. Recent reviews split between praise for remote flexibility (72% fully remote) and complaints about micromanagement and unrealistic targets.

**Leadership (3):** CEO Yamini Rangan has been stable since 2021, and founder Brian Halligan's move to executive chairman completed cleanly in 2025. The more notable signal is CFO Isabelle Winkles' departure, effective May 2026 — a finance-chief exit worth watching but not yet accompanied by other C-suite churn.

**AI strategy (4):** Breeze AI (Assistant, Agents, Intelligence) is a genuinely broad agentic push across the CRM, positioning HubSpot competitively against Salesforce's more fragmented AI/marketing stack.

**RIF risk (3):** No formal mass layoff since January 2023 (500 people, aged out of the 12-24 month window), and headcount is actually up 21.5% YoY. But recent reporting describes "shadow layoffs" — performance-based pruning that has specifically hit middle management in **marketing and engineering**, with some teams merged or eliminated quietly. Active job postings are also down 22% YoY even as total headcount grows — a soft early-warning signal. This directly touches the marketing/ops function.

**Verdict:** Given a moderate-to-stability-leaning weighting (no explicit priority on file — assumed based on job-search history skewing toward Director-level ops/CRM roles), HubSpot is the safer of the three companies evaluated. RIF risk is the swing factor: quieter and slower-moving than the other two, though the marketing-function pruning is real and worth asking about directly in any interview process.

### Klaviyo — 2026-07-23

**Business fundamentals (4):** Klaviyo delivered 28% revenue growth in Q1 2026, its first GAAP-profitable quarter, and raised FY26 guidance to $1.51-1.52B (23% growth). Stock is up 53% over the past year. Caveat: still a young public company (IPO'd 2023), concentrated in SMB/Shopify ecommerce — a more cyclical customer base than HubSpot's broader mix.

**Culture (3):** Glassdoor is 3.4/5, down slightly. Reviews are more pointed than HubSpot's — repeated mentions that leadership is "arrogant" and "driving out" top people, a poorly communicated RTO policy, and AI being "pushed relentlessly." Notably, less than 10% of sales reps hit quota in 2025 — a real warning sign about go-to-market health and comp design.

**Leadership (3):** Chano Fernández (ex-Workday, SAP, Eightfold) joined as co-CEO effective January 1, 2026, taking over go-to-market and operations while founder Andrew Bialecki shifts to focus solely on AI product. This is the kind of new-executive-with-a-mandate transition that often precedes cost discipline — worth watching over the next 2-3 quarters.

**AI strategy (5):** The strongest signal in this set. Klaviyo is repositioning as an "autonomous B2C CRM," shipping Composer and Customer Agent, with the founder now dedicated full-time to AI product — a clearer, more aggressive AI-first bet than HubSpot's or ActiveCampaign's.

**RIF risk (2):** Two distinct layoff rounds in the trailing 12 months: ~100 R&D roles cut in August/September 2025 as part of an AI-driven restructuring, and a no-warning Customer Success & Support layoff in February 2026, timed right before scheduled bonuses. Combined with the new co-CEO transition and reports of leadership pushing out senior people, this is a real pattern — though happening alongside strong financials, suggesting efficiency-driven (AI headcount substitution) rather than distress-driven cuts. CS&S is a function adjacent to marketing/CRM ops backgrounds, so exposure is direct.

**Verdict:** Better growth and AI trajectory than HubSpot, but the RIF signal (two rounds in 12 months, functions adjacent to marketing/ops directly hit) and unresolved leadership transition make it the higher near-term risk of the two. A stability-vs-upside tradeoff, not a clear-cut loser — but if stability is weighted more heavily, HubSpot edges it out.

### ActiveCampaign — 2026-07-23

**Business fundamentals (3):** Private company, last valued at ~$3B in its April 2021 Series C ($240M raised total), backed by Tiger Global, Dragoneer, Susquehanna, and Silversmith. Revenue estimates range $250M (2024) to $320-360M (2024-25 ARR), growing 14-18% YoY — real but slower than both HubSpot and Klaviyo. No funding round in five years means no recent market-based valuation check.

**Culture (2):** Glassdoor sits at 3.2/5, down 6% over the past year — the weakest of the three, with only 46% recommending it to a friend. Recent reviews are unusually blunt, dominated by layoff fallout ("Layoff town," "A Soul-Sucking Sinking Ship"), plus complaints about reactive leadership decisions and an AI pivot that's landed as internally chaotic.

**Leadership (3):** Founder/CEO Jason VandeBoom has stayed in place — a genuine stability anchor. But the layer below him has turned over almost entirely in the past 18 months: new CMO, CRO, CSO (all announced October 2024) and a new CFO. A near-total executive bench refresh combined with the layoffs below reads as a company mid-reset.

**AI strategy (4):** Active Intelligence is deeply embedded rather than bolted-on, won a 2026 AI Excellence Award, and was backed by an acquisition (Feedback Intelligence, Feb 2026) plus new integrations (Google Ads connector, July 2026). A credible, sustained bet, though the internal rollout has reportedly been disruptive.

**RIF risk (1):** Multiple sources point to a ~15% layoff including director and VP-level departures, hitting Sales hardest, described as abrupt and without warning, as recently as around December 2025. Layered on top: a stagnant private valuation, growth trailing peers, a nearly full C-suite refresh, and a hiring pipeline shrunk to roughly 9 open roles for a 500-1,000 person org. Every leading indicator points the same direction — a genuine red flag.

**Verdict:** The highest-risk of the three companies evaluated. The AI product story is real, but it doesn't offset a confirmed, recent, director/VP-level layoff round, a stalled valuation, and a nearly dry hiring pipeline. If stability matters, this is the one to be most cautious about.
